# University Career Services Synthetic Media Policy

**Policy owner:** Director of Career Services  
**Applies to:** Career Services employees, student employees, contractors, vendors, interns, and partner units producing content on behalf of Career Services  
**Effective date:** Upon adoption  
**Review cycle:** Every 12 months, or sooner following a material incident or major technology change

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs Career Services' creation, procurement, publication, and reuse of realistic AI-generated or AI-altered audio, video, and human representations. Its purpose is to allow narrowly defined, low-risk uses while protecting students, employees, partners, and the university from impersonation, misinformation, privacy loss, false endorsement, and unaccountable advice.

Synthetic media is not prohibited merely because it uses AI. It is prohibited or restricted when it creates a misleading human presence, borrows a real person's identity without meaningful consent, obscures responsibility, or delivers guidance whose consequences require individualized professional judgment.

Every approved synthetic-media item must have an identifiable human owner. A synthetic presenter may deliver a message, but it may never become the accountable source of that message.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies when Career Services creates, commissions, edits, purchases, publishes, distributes, or materially relies upon:

- AI-generated or cloned voices;
- realistic synthetic presenters, avatars, faces, or bodies;
- lip-synced or dubbed media that makes a person appear to say words they did not record;
- materially altered recordings that change the meaning, speaker identity, or apparent circumstances;
- personalized synthetic audio or video generated from student or employee data; or
- third-party synthetic media reposted through an official Career Services channel.

Routine accessibility and production tools are outside this policy when they do not create a misleading representation. Examples include caption generation with human correction, background-noise removal, color correction, formatting, and clearly non-realistic animation. Normal editing becomes covered when it materially changes what a person appears to have said or done.

## 3. Definitions

**Synthetic media:** Audio, video, or imagery substantially generated or altered by AI so that it realistically represents a person, voice, event, or performance that did not occur as depicted.

**Identifiable person:** A person who can reasonably be recognized through face, voice, name, distinctive behavior, biography, or a combination of characteristics.

**Human owner:** The Career Services employee who verifies the content, approves the final output, retains supporting records, and accepts responsibility for correction or withdrawal.

**High-stakes guidance:** Information that could materially affect immigration status, legal rights, safety, finances, eligibility, employment decisions, academic standing, or access to university services.

**Meaningful consent:** Specific, informed, documented, and voluntary authorization for a defined synthetic use. General photography, recording, or publicity consent does not constitute permission to clone a likeness or voice.

## 4. Governing Principles

Career Services will apply the following rules to every covered use:

1. **Human accountability:** A named employee and approving official must stand behind every message.
2. **Truth before production:** Scripts and factual claims must be verified before synthetic media is generated.
3. **Consent before identity use:** Possession of recordings does not authorize cloning.
4. **Disclosure by design:** Disclosure must travel within the media, not only in a caption or filename.
5. **Minimum necessary realism:** The office will not use a realistic human representation when text, graphics, ordinary narration, or a clearly non-realistic avatar would achieve the same purpose with lower risk.
6. **No automated trust:** Detection results, provider safeguards, or platform labels do not replace human review.
7. **Refusal is an acceptable outcome:** Career Services will decline projects whose risks cannot be reduced to an acceptable level.

## 5. Permitted Uses

The following uses may be approved when all requirements in this policy are satisfied:

- A fully synthetic, non-identifiable presenter delivering low-risk, general information such as office hours, event reminders, navigation instructions, or explanations of publicly available services.
- Translation or dubbing of an approved informational script when the synthetic voice does not imitate an identifiable person and a qualified reviewer verifies meaning and pronunciation.
- A clearly non-realistic avatar used for orientation, software demonstrations, or practice exercises.
- Synthetic narration of university-approved public information when the narration is disclosed and does not imply endorsement by a real employee.
- Internal prototypes used only by the approved project team and marked “NOT FOR PUBLICATION.”
- Accessibility support, such as a synthetic reading of approved text, when users are informed that the voice is generated and a non-synthetic alternative is available where reasonably possible.

Permitted use does not mean automatic approval. Every public item still requires the review process in Section 9.

## 6. Prohibited Uses

Career Services will not create, commission, publish, or knowingly distribute synthetic media that:

- uses a real person's voice, face, likeness, or identifiable characteristics without meaningful consent;
- depicts a university leader, professor, employer representative, recruiter, student, alum, or Career Services employee saying or doing something they did not record, except under the narrowly controlled consent process in Section 7;
- fabricates testimonials, employer statements, student outcomes, hiring commitments, quotations, events, or endorsements;
- presents high-stakes or individualized immigration, legal, financial, mental-health, or safety guidance through an autonomous synthetic presenter;
- impersonates an employer or suggests that an employer has approved a message when it has not;
- conceals that a realistic presenter or voice is synthetic;
- uses student data to generate personalized media without documented authorization, a defined educational purpose, and privacy review;
- is designed to manipulate emotion through a fabricated crisis, threat, authority figure, or personal relationship;
- removes, hides, crops, or weakens required disclosure or provenance information;
- attempts to evade platform safeguards, detection systems, or content rules;
- uses synthetic media to evaluate candidates or students based on inferred emotion, personality, honesty, disability, accent, or other sensitive characteristics; or
- would remain misleading even if labeled synthetic.

Disclosure does not convert a prohibited use into a permitted one.

## 7. Consent Requirements

Career Services may use an identifiable person's voice or likeness only when the Director and the university's designated privacy or legal reviewer approve a written consent agreement before model creation or generation begins.

The agreement must state:

- the specific person and identity elements being used;
- the approved purpose, scripts or subject areas, audiences, and distribution channels;
- whether a reusable model, clone, or vendor profile will be created;
- the responsible university owner and any vendors receiving source files;
- the start date, expiration date, and storage period;
- whether the person will review each output or only defined categories;
- how consent may be revoked and what happens to previously published items;
- whether compensation applies; and
- how source recordings, models, and generated outputs will be deleted.

Consent must be separate from ordinary employment paperwork and standard media releases. Silence, continued employment, participation in an event, or previous permission to record does not establish consent. Student employees and other individuals in a dependent relationship must be offered a genuine non-synthetic alternative without retaliation.

Revocation stops new generation immediately. The human owner must assess existing content within five business days and remove it where contractually and operationally possible. If removal is impossible, the office must document why and add corrective disclosure where feasible.

## 8. Disclosure and Provenance Standards

All public-facing realistic synthetic media must contain clear disclosure inside the asset. The minimum statement is:

> This content uses an AI-generated presenter and/or voice. Career Services reviewed and approved the message.

For video, disclosure must appear visibly at the beginning and remain as a persistent indicator or return at the end. For audio-only content, the acknowledgment must be spoken near the beginning. The post caption, description, transcript, and accessible text alternative must repeat the disclosure.

Disclosure must identify what is synthetic. Vague statements such as “created with AI” are insufficient when the presenter, voice, or apparent performance is generated.

When supported by the production and publishing systems, the human owner must preserve Content Credentials or equivalent provenance records. The project file must also contain:

- the final approved script;
- generation tool and version, when available;
- creation and approval dates;
- source and consent records;
- names of the human owner and approvers;
- final published file and cryptographic checksum where feasible; and
- publication locations.

Provenance is supporting evidence, not proof that the message is true. Missing credentials must not be treated as proof that content is fake, and valid credentials must not substitute for factual review.

## 9. Review and Approval Workflow

Before production, the requester must submit a short project record describing the purpose, audience, platform, proposed synthetic elements, data used, risks, and why a lower-risk format is insufficient.

Approval levels are:

**Level 1 — Low-risk, non-identifiable use:** General public information using a generic synthetic voice, clearly non-realistic avatar, or non-identifiable presenter. Requires approval from the content owner and communications lead.

**Level 2 — Realistic or personalized use:** Realistic synthetic presenter, public-facing synthetic voice, translation, or limited personalization. Requires the Director's approval plus privacy review. Accessibility and subject-matter review must be added when applicable.

**Level 3 — Identifiable-person use:** Any approved cloning or synthetic representation of a real person. Requires written consent, Director approval, privacy or legal approval, information-security review of vendors and storage, and the person's review of the final output.

High-stakes individualized guidance, deceptive impersonation, and all uses listed in Section 6 are not eligible for approval.

Reviewers must confirm:

- the script is accurate, current, and within Career Services' authority;
- the audience is unlikely to misunderstand who is speaking;
- consent is valid where required;
- data use is necessary and authorized;
- disclosure is visible, audible, accessible, and difficult to separate from the asset;
- provenance and records will be preserved;
- a non-synthetic option was considered;
- platform rules are satisfied; and
- the office can correct or withdraw the content if necessary.

Material changes to the script, identity, audience, platform, or degree of realism require renewed approval.

## 10. Publication, Retention, and Vendor Controls

Approved media must be published first through an official university-controlled account or webpage. Staff may not publish covered content from personal accounts on behalf of Career Services.

The human owner must retain the project record, approvals, consent, script, final asset, and publication history according to university records requirements and for no less than three years after the last official use, unless a longer period is required.

Before using a vendor, Career Services must confirm that the vendor's terms address data ownership, model training, subcontractors, security, deletion, breach notification, and reuse of uploaded recordings. Sensitive or restricted university data may not be entered into a public generative service unless the university has approved the service for that data classification.

Source recordings and reusable voice or likeness models must be access-controlled. They may not be retained merely because storage is convenient.

## 11. Incident Response

An incident includes inaccurate synthetic content, missing disclosure, unauthorized identity use, leaked source material, vendor misuse, misleading redistribution, or public confusion about whether Career Services endorsed a message.

Anyone discovering an incident must immediately preserve relevant evidence and notify the Director and the university's privacy, communications, and information-security contacts. The response owner must:

1. pause scheduled publication and disable official copies where possible;
2. preserve the original files, prompts, approvals, logs, URLs, timestamps, and screenshots;
3. assess affected people, platforms, audiences, and potential high-stakes consequences;
4. notify the represented person and other affected parties when appropriate;
5. request correction, labeling, or removal from platforms and partner accounts;
6. publish a correction through the same channels used for the original content when silence would leave a misleading impression;
7. provide affected students with a human contact and corrected guidance;
8. investigate the cause and document actions taken; and
9. determine whether production must remain suspended pending policy, vendor, or control changes.

The office must not rely exclusively on automated detection when deciding whether a reported item is authentic. Verification should use retained project records, official publication history, provenance where available, and human investigation.

## 12. Refusal Conditions

Career Services must decline a project when:

- the identity holder does not provide meaningful consent;
- the message cannot be verified or responsibly qualified;
- the audience may interpret a synthetic speaker as a real advisor or institutional official;
- the project involves prohibited or high-stakes individualized guidance;
- disclosure would materially undermine the project's stated purpose;
- the project depends on removing or minimizing disclosure;
- the vendor cannot provide acceptable privacy, security, ownership, or deletion terms;
- the office cannot maintain a human owner or respond to foreseeable misuse;
- a less realistic format would meet the legitimate communication need with substantially lower risk; or
- the anticipated benefit does not justify the residual risk.

Deadlines, cost savings, engagement goals, or the fact that a tool permits generation are not sufficient reasons to override refusal.

## 13. Training, Audit, and Enforcement

Employees and contractors involved in covered work must complete annual training on synthetic-media identification, consent, disclosure, privacy, accessibility, records, and incident reporting.

The Director will maintain a register of approved projects and conduct a semiannual audit of active and sampled archived items. The audit will examine approval records, consent status, disclosure, provenance, vendor controls, publication locations, corrections, and retention or deletion obligations.

Noncompliant content must be paused or removed until corrected. Repeated or intentional violations may result in loss of publishing privileges, vendor termination, contract remedies, or employee discipline under applicable university procedures. Good-faith reporting of suspected violations is protected from retaliation.

## 14. Exceptions and Policy Review

No exception may authorize a prohibited use. Other exceptions must be documented in writing by the Director and the university's designated privacy or legal reviewer, including the business need, duration, additional safeguards, and expiration date.

This policy will be reviewed at least annually against incidents, audit findings, technology changes, platform requirements, relevant law, and student feedback. Continued use of a previously approved tool or project is not automatic evidence that it remains acceptable.
