# Case Study Analysis: Synthetic Media in University Career Services

## 1. Introduction

As universities adopt generative AI tools, synthetic media could become part of student-facing services such as career advising, interview preparation, resume guidance, and employer engagement.

This case study examines a hypothetical university career services department that wants to use AI-generated avatars and synthetic video content to provide personalized career guidance to students.

The goal of this case study is to apply the ethical principles and governance framework developed in Phase A and Phase B to a realistic organizational use case.

---

## 2. Scenario

A university career services office wants to introduce an AI-powered virtual career coach.

The system would use synthetic media to generate personalized video guidance for students based on information such as:

- Degree program
- Resume content
- Career interests
- Internship experience
- Preferred industries
- Interview preparation needs

The AI-generated virtual advisor could provide:

- Resume feedback
- Mock interview guidance
- Career path recommendations
- Networking advice
- Job search strategies
- Personalized career-development videos

The university expects this system to reduce repetitive advising work and improve access to career support.

---

## 3. Potential Benefits

Synthetic media could provide several operational and student-facing benefits.

### Scalability

Career centers often support thousands of students with limited staff capacity.

AI-generated content could allow the university to provide guidance outside normal office hours and reduce repetitive advising tasks.

### Personalization

Instead of providing the same generic career resources to all students, the system could generate guidance based on individual student profiles.

### Accessibility

Synthetic media could support:

- Multiple languages
- Captioning
- Audio-based guidance
- Different presentation formats

This could improve accessibility for international students and students with different learning preferences.

### Consistency

Automated guidance could help ensure that students receive consistent baseline information about resumes, interviews, and job-search practices.

---

## 4. Ethical Risks

Despite the potential benefits, the use of synthetic media introduces several ethical concerns.

### 4.1 Transparency

Students must clearly understand that they are interacting with AI-generated content.

If the synthetic advisor appears highly realistic, students may incorrectly assume they are receiving advice from a real university employee.

The university should therefore provide clear disclosure whenever synthetic media is used.

Example disclosure:

> "This career guidance video was generated using artificial intelligence and has been reviewed by the university career services team."

---

### 4.2 Accuracy and Hallucination Risk

AI-generated career advice may include inaccurate information.

For example, the system could provide incorrect information about:

- Employer requirements
- Salary expectations
- Immigration or work authorization
- Recruiting timelines
- Application procedures

Incorrect information could negatively affect a student's career decisions.

Because of this risk, high-impact guidance should require human review.

---

### 4.3 Privacy

Personalization may require the system to process sensitive student information.

Examples include:

- Resumes
- Academic history
- Employment history
- Contact details
- Career preferences
- Immigration or work authorization information

The university should minimize the amount of student data used by the AI system and clearly define how that information is stored and processed.

---

### 4.4 Consent

Students should understand how their information is being used.

The university should obtain consent before using student data to generate personalized synthetic content.

Students should also have the option to use traditional career services instead of the AI system.

---

### 4.5 Bias

AI-generated recommendations may reflect biases present in training data.

For example, the system could unintentionally recommend different career paths based on demographic characteristics or provide lower-quality guidance for students from less represented backgrounds.

Bias testing should therefore be included before and after deployment.

---

### 4.6 Over-Reliance on Automation

Students may begin treating AI-generated advice as authoritative.

Career decisions are often complex and depend on individual circumstances.

The system should therefore be positioned as a support tool rather than a replacement for professional career advisors.

---

## 5. Risk Assessment

The following table evaluates major risks associated with the proposed system.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Risk Level | Recommended Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incorrect career guidance | Medium | High | High | Human review and source validation |
| Undisclosed AI-generated content | Medium | Medium | Medium | Mandatory AI disclosure |
| Student data exposure | Low | High | High | Data minimization and access controls |
| Biased recommendations | Medium | High | High | Bias testing and monitoring |
| Unauthorized use of likeness | Low | High | High | Written consent |
| Student over-reliance on AI | Medium | Medium | Medium | Clear limitations and escalation to human advisors |
| Outdated information | Medium | Medium | Medium | Regular content review and update cycles |

---

## 6. Applying the Phase B Governance Framework

The governance framework developed in Phase B can be applied directly to this case.

### Disclosure Requirement

All AI-generated videos should clearly disclose that synthetic media was used.

### Human Oversight

Career services staff should review high-impact or personalized content before it is delivered to students.

### Consent

Students should explicitly consent to the use of their personal information for AI-based personalization.

### Data Governance

Only the minimum information required to generate the service should be collected and processed.

### Approval Process

New synthetic-media use cases should be reviewed before deployment.

This review should involve representatives from:

- Career services
- Information technology
- Legal or compliance
- Data privacy
- Accessibility
- Student services

### Incident Response

The university should maintain procedures for handling incidents such as:

- Incorrect guidance
- Privacy breaches
- Biased recommendations
- Misuse of synthetic media
- Student complaints

---

## 7. Decision Framework

The following decision process could be used before synthetic media is deployed:

1. Identify the purpose of the synthetic content.
2. Determine whether personal data is required.
3. Evaluate potential risks to students.
4. Confirm that appropriate disclosure is included.
5. Determine whether human review is required.
6. Verify that consent has been obtained.
7. Complete bias and accuracy testing.
8. Approve or reject the use case.
9. Monitor the system after deployment.

---

## 8. Example Use Cases

### Use Case 1: General Resume Tips

The university creates an AI-generated video explaining basic resume formatting.

**Decision:** Low risk.

**Recommended action:** Allow with AI disclosure and periodic content review.

---

### Use Case 2: Personalized Interview Coaching

The system generates interview advice based on a student's resume and target job.

**Decision:** Medium risk.

**Recommended action:** Allow with student consent, privacy controls, and human oversight.

---

### Use Case 3: Immigration and Work Authorization Guidance

The AI system provides specific advice about visas or employment authorization.

**Decision:** High risk.

**Recommended action:** Do not allow fully automated guidance. Students should be directed to qualified university staff or authorized professionals.

---

### Use Case 4: Synthetic University Employee

The university creates an AI avatar that closely resembles a real career advisor without clearly informing students.

**Decision:** High risk.

**Recommended action:** Reject unless the individual provides explicit consent and the synthetic nature of the content is clearly disclosed.

---

## 9. Recommended Safeguards

Before implementing synthetic media in career services, the university should establish the following safeguards:

- Mandatory AI disclosure
- Human review for high-impact guidance
- Clear consent mechanisms
- Data minimization
- Bias testing
- Regular accuracy audits
- Content version tracking
- Student feedback mechanisms
- Escalation to human advisors
- Incident response procedures

---

## 10. Key Findings

This case study demonstrates that synthetic media can provide significant operational benefits while also introducing ethical and governance risks.

The most significant risks involve:

- Accuracy
- Privacy
- Bias
- Transparency
- Consent
- Accountability

The analysis also shows that risk is highly dependent on the specific use case.

A general AI-generated resume video creates significantly less risk than personalized immigration or employment advice.

For this reason, organizations should evaluate synthetic-media systems based on the potential impact of each individual use case rather than treating all AI-generated content as having the same level of risk.

---

## 11. Conclusion

Synthetic media could expand access to university career services and reduce repetitive workloads for staff.

However, the technology should not be deployed solely because it is technically possible.

Organizations should evaluate whether the use of synthetic media is appropriate, transparent, accurate, and proportionate to the potential risks.

The governance framework developed in Phase B provides a structured approach for making these decisions.

This case study demonstrates how ethical principles can be translated into practical organizational controls for responsible AI deployment.
