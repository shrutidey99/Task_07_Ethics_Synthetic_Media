# Governance Audit and Compliance Checklist

## Purpose

This governance audit evaluates whether a university Career Services office is complying with the synthetic-media policy developed in Phase B of this project.

The policy establishes requirements for human accountability, factual accuracy, consent, disclosure, provenance, approval, vendor management, publication, incident response, and record retention. This audit converts those requirements into measurable checks.

The goal is not only to confirm whether documents exist. It is to determine whether synthetic-media projects are being created and managed responsibly in practice.

## Audit Objectives

The audit has five objectives:

1. Verify that every synthetic-media project has an accountable human owner.
2. Confirm that permitted, restricted, and prohibited uses are classified correctly.
3. Check whether consent, disclosure, provenance, and approval requirements are followed.
4. Identify policy violations and weak controls before they cause harm.
5. Require corrective action and document whether identified problems are resolved.

## Audit Scope

The audit applies to synthetic-media projects created, purchased, commissioned, published, or distributed by Career Services.

This includes:

- AI-generated presenters and avatars
- Synthetic or cloned voices
- AI-generated career-advice videos
- Lip-synced or dubbed recordings
- Personalized audio or video
- Multilingual synthetic content
- Third-party synthetic media shared through official channels
- Vendors producing synthetic media for Career Services

Routine captioning, noise removal, color correction, and formatting are outside the audit unless they materially change what a person appears to have said or done.

## Audit Frequency

A formal audit should be conducted every six months.

An additional audit should be triggered when:

- A synthetic-media incident is reported
- A new generation tool or vendor is adopted
- A real person’s voice or likeness is used
- A project includes student personalization
- A disclosure or consent failure is discovered
- A significant policy or legal change occurs
- Synthetic content is distributed through a new platform

## Audit Sample

The auditor should review:

- Every active Level 3 project involving an identifiable person
- Every project involving student personalization
- Every reported incident
- Every approved policy exception
- At least 25% of active Level 1 and Level 2 projects
- At least 10% of archived projects published during the audit period

The auditor may expand the sample when early findings indicate repeated or systemic problems.

## Required Evidence

Each project should maintain an evidence file containing:

- Project title and purpose
- Intended audience
- Name of the human owner
- Final approved script
- Factual verification record
- Risk classification
- Required approvals
- Consent documentation, when applicable
- Privacy and security review
- Vendor assessment
- Tool and model information
- Disclosure language
- Final published media
- Content Credentials or other provenance records, when supported
- Publication channels and URLs
- Creation and approval dates
- Retention and deletion schedule
- Incident or correction records
- Documentation showing that a lower-risk alternative was considered

A missing record should be treated as an audit finding, even when an employee states that the required action occurred.

# Compliance Checklist

## 1. Project Ownership and Accountability

- [ ] The project has a named human owner.
- [ ] The human owner is a Career Services employee with authority over the content.
- [ ] The owner verified the final script and media.
- [ ] The approving official is identified.
- [ ] Contact information exists for corrections or complaints.
- [ ] Responsibility is assigned for monitoring the content after publication.
- [ ] Responsibility is assigned for withdrawal or correction if necessary.

### Evidence

- Project record
- Approval log
- Employee name and role
- Publication record
- Correction contact

### Automatic Failure Conditions

The project automatically fails this section if:

- No human owner is identified
- Responsibility is assigned only to a vendor or AI system
- No employee accepts responsibility for correcting the content

## 2. Use Classification

- [ ] The synthetic-media use is clearly described.
- [ ] The project is classified as Level 1, Level 2, or Level 3.
- [ ] The classification matches the policy’s risk definitions.
- [ ] The project is not included in a prohibited-use category.
- [ ] The audience is unlikely to mistake the synthetic speaker for a real advisor.
- [ ] A lower-risk format was considered.
- [ ] The use of realistic synthetic representation is necessary and justified.

### Evidence

- Risk-classification form
- Project proposal
- Alternative-format assessment
- Approval decision

### Automatic Failure Conditions

The project automatically fails if it:

- Fabricates a testimonial, endorsement, employer statement, or student outcome
- Uses synthetic media for high-stakes individualized legal, immigration, financial, or safety guidance
- Impersonates an employer, university official, student, or employee
- Remains misleading even after disclosure

## 3. Script Accuracy and Content Review

- [ ] The final script is retained.
- [ ] Factual claims were checked before generation.
- [ ] A qualified subject-matter reviewer approved the content.
- [ ] Sources and review dates are documented.
- [ ] Time-sensitive information has an expiration or review date.
- [ ] The generated output matches the approved script.
- [ ] Translation or dubbing was reviewed by a qualified language reviewer.
- [ ] The content avoids unsupported promises or guarantees.
- [ ] The content remains within Career Services’ authority.

### Evidence

- Approved script
- Sources
- Reviewer notes
- Version history
- Translation verification

### Audit Question

Could a student make an important academic, employment, immigration, financial, or personal decision based on this message?

If yes, the auditor should apply additional scrutiny and determine whether synthetic delivery is appropriate at all.

## 4. Consent and Identity Use

- [ ] The project does not use an identifiable person without written consent.
- [ ] Consent is separate from ordinary employment or media-release paperwork.
- [ ] The identity elements covered by consent are identified.
- [ ] The authorized purpose is documented.
- [ ] Approved scripts or subject areas are defined.
- [ ] Audiences and distribution channels are specified.
- [ ] The consent period has not expired.
- [ ] The individual understood whether a reusable model would be created.
- [ ] Vendor access to source recordings is disclosed.
- [ ] Revocation procedures are documented.
- [ ] Deletion requirements are documented.
- [ ] A genuine non-synthetic alternative was offered.
- [ ] The represented person reviewed the final output when required.

### Evidence

- Signed consent agreement
- Final-output approval
- Model-creation records
- Revocation procedure
- Deletion record

### Automatic Failure Conditions

The project automatically fails if:

- Consent cannot be produced
- Consent is assumed from employment or participation in a recorded event
- The use exceeds the agreed purpose, audience, duration, or platform
- Generation continues after consent has been revoked

## 5. Privacy and Student Data

- [ ] The project uses only the minimum data necessary.
- [ ] The purpose of using student data is documented.
- [ ] Privacy approval was obtained.
- [ ] Students were informed of the data use.
- [ ] Sensitive data was not entered into an unapproved public tool.
- [ ] Personalization does not create the appearance of individualized professional judgment.
- [ ] Data access is restricted to authorized personnel.
- [ ] Vendor data use is documented.
- [ ] Data-retention and deletion periods are defined.
- [ ] The project does not infer sensitive characteristics.

### Evidence

- Privacy review
- Data inventory
- Access-control record
- Vendor agreement
- Retention schedule

### Automatic Failure Conditions

The project fails if it uses student data without authorization or uploads restricted data to an unapproved service.

## 6. Disclosure

- [ ] The media clearly states that it uses an AI-generated presenter or voice.
- [ ] The disclosure identifies which elements are synthetic.
- [ ] Video disclosure appears within the video itself.
- [ ] Audio disclosure is spoken near the beginning.
- [ ] The disclosure is included in the caption or description.
- [ ] The transcript and accessible alternative repeat the disclosure.
- [ ] The disclosure is understandable to the intended audience.
- [ ] The disclosure remains visible or audible after normal reposting.
- [ ] The disclosure has not been cropped, hidden, minimized, or weakened.
- [ ] Career Services is identified as the organization responsible for the message.

### Minimum Acceptable Disclosure

> This content uses an AI-generated presenter and/or voice. Career Services reviewed and approved the message.

### Automatic Failure Conditions

The project fails if:

- A realistic synthetic presenter is not disclosed
- The only disclosure appears in a filename
- The disclosure uses vague language that does not explain what was generated
- A project team intentionally removes or minimizes the disclosure

## 7. Provenance and Documentation

- [ ] The generation tool is documented.
- [ ] The model or version is recorded when available.
- [ ] Creation and approval dates are retained.
- [ ] Prompts or production instructions are retained when appropriate.
- [ ] The final approved file is preserved.
- [ ] Publication locations are documented.
- [ ] Content Credentials are preserved when supported.
- [ ] A checksum or comparable integrity record exists when feasible.
- [ ] Re-encoded versions are tracked.
- [ ] Provenance is not treated as proof that the underlying message is true.

### Evidence

- Production log
- Content Credentials
- File checksum
- Publication history
- Final asset archive

### Audit Limitation

The absence of Content Credentials does not prove that content is false. Valid credentials also do not prove that the script is accurate. Provenance should be treated as one layer of evidence.

## 8. Review and Approval

- [ ] Approval occurred before public production or publication.
- [ ] The approval level matches the project’s risk.
- [ ] All required reviewers participated.
- [ ] The communications lead approved Level 1 content.
- [ ] The Director and privacy reviewer approved Level 2 content.
- [ ] Legal, privacy, security, and identity-holder approvals exist for Level 3 content.
- [ ] Accessibility review was completed when applicable.
- [ ] Material changes received renewed approval.
- [ ] The final published version matches the approved version.
- [ ] No prohibited use was approved as an exception.

### Evidence

- Approval form
- Reviewer names
- Dates
- Version history
- Exception record

## 9. Vendor Governance

- [ ] The vendor received a privacy and security review.
- [ ] The contract defines data ownership.
- [ ] The contract explains whether uploaded content may be used for model training.
- [ ] Subcontractors are disclosed.
- [ ] Security requirements are documented.
- [ ] Breach-notification obligations are defined.
- [ ] Data-deletion requirements are enforceable.
- [ ] The vendor cannot reuse voices, faces, or recordings for unrelated purposes.
- [ ] The university can export or delete project records.
- [ ] Vendor access is removed when the project ends.

### Evidence

- Contract
- Security review
- Privacy terms
- Deletion confirmation
- Vendor access log

### Automatic Failure Conditions

The project fails if the vendor can reuse an identifiable person’s recordings or model without explicit authorization.

## 10. Publication and Accessibility

- [ ] Content was first published through an official university-controlled channel.
- [ ] Staff did not use personal accounts as the official source.
- [ ] Captions or transcripts are accurate.
- [ ] Visual disclosures are accessible.
- [ ] Audio information has a text alternative.
- [ ] The published media matches the approved file.
- [ ] Platforms’ synthetic-media disclosure tools were used.
- [ ] Comments or reports are monitored for confusion or misuse.
- [ ] The office can remove or correct the content.

### Evidence

- Official URL
- Screenshots
- Platform settings
- Accessibility review
- Publication date

## 11. Retention and Deletion

- [ ] Records are retained for the required period.
- [ ] Consent expiration dates are monitored.
- [ ] Source recordings are access-controlled.
- [ ] Reusable voice or likeness models are access-controlled.
- [ ] Unnecessary source material is deleted.
- [ ] Vendor deletion is confirmed.
- [ ] Revoked consent triggers review of published content.
- [ ] Archived copies remain properly labeled.
- [ ] Retention does not continue merely for convenience.

## 12. Incident Response

- [ ] Employees know how to report an incident.
- [ ] The office maintains an incident-response contact list.
- [ ] Evidence-preservation procedures exist.
- [ ] Publication can be paused quickly.
- [ ] Removal and correction procedures are documented.
- [ ] Affected individuals can be notified.
- [ ] Corrected guidance can be issued through the original channels.
- [ ] Students can contact a real advisor for assistance.
- [ ] Root-cause analysis is completed.
- [ ] Lessons from incidents are incorporated into policy and training.

# Audit Scoring Method

Each applicable item receives one of the following scores:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 2 | Fully compliant and supported by evidence |
| 1 | Partially compliant or documentation incomplete |
| 0 | Noncompliant or no evidence available |
| N/A | Not applicable to the project |

The compliance percentage is calculated as:

> Points earned ÷ total applicable points × 100

## Compliance Ratings

| Score | Rating | Required Response |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100% | Compliant | Continue with routine monitoring |
| 75–89% | Needs improvement | Correct minor findings within 30 days |
| 50–74% | Significant deficiencies | Pause new publication and correct within 15 days |
| Below 50% | Critical noncompliance | Suspend the project immediately |
| Any automatic failure | Critical noncompliance | Suspend or remove the content regardless of total score |

A strong overall percentage cannot override an automatic failure involving prohibited content, missing consent, unauthorized student data, deceptive impersonation, or absent disclosure.

# Finding Severity

## Critical

A violation that creates immediate risk to identity, privacy, safety, legal rights, immigration status, employment decisions, or institutional trust.

Examples:

- Unauthorized voice cloning
- Fabricated employer endorsement
- Undisclosed realistic presenter
- Synthetic immigration guidance
- Use of restricted student data in a public tool

Required response: Stop publication immediately and begin incident response.

## High

A major control failure that could result in harm if not corrected.

Examples:

- Missing privacy review
- Incorrect approval level
- Vendor contract without deletion protections
- Expired consent
- Published content that differs from the approved script

Required response: Pause the project and correct the finding within 15 days.

## Medium

A weakness that reduces accountability or traceability.

Examples:

- Incomplete production log
- Missing publication record
- Weak disclosure placement
- Missing model-version information

Required response: Correct within 30 days.

## Low

An administrative issue with limited immediate risk.

Examples:

- Inconsistent file naming
- Minor formatting problem
- Outdated contact information

Required response: Correct during the next routine update.

# Corrective-Action Record

Each finding should include:

- Finding number
- Project name
- Policy requirement
- Evidence reviewed
- Description of the problem
- Risk severity
- Responsible owner
- Required corrective action
- Due date
- Completion evidence
- Verification date
- Final status

Possible statuses are:

- Open
- In progress
- Resolved
- Risk accepted
- Project suspended
- Content removed

Risk acceptance cannot be used for prohibited uses or automatic failures.

# Worked Audit Example

## Project

AI-generated video promoting a Career Services résumé workshop.

## Proposed Use

A generic synthetic presenter announces the workshop date, location, registration link, and intended audience. The video is posted on the university website and social media.

## Audit Findings

| Control Area | Finding | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Human ownership | Communications coordinator identified | Compliant |
| Use classification | Classified as Level 1 general information | Compliant |
| Script accuracy | Workshop details verified | Compliant |
| Consent | Presenter is fully synthetic and non-identifiable | Not applicable |
| Student data | No personal data used | Compliant |
| Disclosure | Opening disclosure present, but absent from caption | Partially compliant |
| Provenance | Tool and final file documented | Compliant |
| Approval | Communications lead approved the script and video | Compliant |
| Vendor review | Approved university tool used | Compliant |
| Accessibility | Captions included and reviewed | Compliant |
| Retention | Project record retained | Compliant |
| Incident response | Human contact included on event page | Compliant |

## Result

The project is mostly compliant, but publication should be corrected to include disclosure in the social-media caption and accessible description.

## Corrective Action

Add the following statement to every publication caption:

> This video uses an AI-generated presenter. Career Services reviewed and approved the message.

The human owner should confirm the correction and update the project record.

# Final Assessment

A synthetic-media policy becomes meaningful only when compliance can be demonstrated. This audit framework makes the policy measurable by connecting each requirement to evidence, scoring, corrective action, and accountability.

The audit cannot guarantee that synthetic content will never be misunderstood or misused. It can establish whether Career Services followed its own safeguards, identify where controls are failing, and require action before convenience or speed overrides responsible governance.
