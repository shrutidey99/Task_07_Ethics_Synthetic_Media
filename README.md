# Task 07: Ethics of Synthetic Media

## Overview

This repository contains my work for Research Task 7: The Ethics of Synthetic Representation. The project builds directly on the synthetic audio and video artifacts I created in Task 6.

Task 7 moves from creating synthetic media to examining how organizations should govern it. Phase A analyzes the ethical implications of synthetic representation across the dimensions of truthfulness, consent, context, and scale. Phase B will translate that analysis into an operational synthetic-media policy.

## Organizational Context

The organizational setting selected for this project is a university career services office.

This context was chosen because my Task 6 artifacts used synthetic presenters to deliver career guidance to graduate students. Although the original script was truthful, the project demonstrated how a realistic voice, professional setting, and human-looking presenter can create authority without a real speaker being personally accountable for the message.

A university career services office must therefore establish clear boundaries for synthetic media, particularly when communicating information that may affect employment, immigration-related decisions, professional opportunities, or student trust.

## Phase A Summary

Phase A examines how the ethical character of synthetic media changes across four axes:

1. **Truth:** A realistic synthetic presenter delivers fabricated information.
2. **Consent:** A real person's likeness or voice is used without meaningful authorization.
3. **Context:** Disclosure and provenance disappear when content is edited or redistributed.
4. **Scale:** Synthetic messages are generated and personalized in large quantities.

The analysis also evaluates:

- Disclosure and labeling
- C2PA Content Credentials
- Automated detection
- Legal and regulatory approaches
- Platform policies
- Professional and organizational norms

Each mitigation provides useful protection but has identifiable failure points. The analysis concludes that synthetic media requires layered controls and identifiable human accountability.

## Reflection

The most surprising lesson from Task 6 was that producing a credible human presence required very little specialized expertise. Although the video tool was limited to ten-second outputs, it successfully generated professional-looking speakers with stable settings, natural body language, and distinct personalities.

The central ethical concern is therefore not simply whether synthetic content is visibly fake. It is that synthetic media can separate the authority of a human voice and face from the accountability normally attached to a real speaker.
